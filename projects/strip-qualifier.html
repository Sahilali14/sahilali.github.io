Strip Qualifier — Automated QC System for Aztec Code Inspection

Designed an automated conveyor-based quality-control machine for inspecting strips with Aztec codes. The system feeds, aligns and inspects each strip, and automatically rejects failed strips into a dedicated bin, replacing a largely manual QC process.

I was involved across design, manufacturing, assembly, testing and validation, taking the machine from concept to a validated solution within 3 months while managing other engineering responsibilities in parallel.

Engineering Focus

Conveyor-based automation, machine-vision fixturing, adjustable inspection mechanisms, automatic rejection, and design for replication.

4K → 15K QC throughput increased from 4K to 15K strips/day
3.75× Improvement in daily inspection throughput
3 months From concept to a validated solution
3 machines Additional machines built from the validated design

My Contribution

  • Conveyor-based mechanical system: Designed the complete mechanical system, including strip feeding, alignment, inspection and automatic rejection mechanisms.
  • Adjustable inspection structures: Designed adjustable mechanical structures for the camera, dome light and sensors, providing 3 degrees of freedom for accurate positioning and setup.
  • Sorting mechanism: Designed the mechanism that automatically diverts failed strips into a dedicated rejection bin.
  • Sourcing and manufacturing: Selected and sourced components and coordinated with external manufacturing vendors for fabrication.
  • Assembly and troubleshooting: Worked closely with the assembly team to support machine build, integration and troubleshooting.
  • Replication: After successful validation, supported the development of three additional machines based on the validated design.

Impact

Increased QC throughput from 4K to 15K strips/day, a 3.75× improvement, while reducing manual QC requirements and improving the inspection workflow. The machine was delivered from concept to a validated solution within 3 months, and its successful validation enabled the design to be replicated for three additional production machines.
